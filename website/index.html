A little closer
to your flow.

Lunlet sits beside your Mac's notch and keeps the coding sessions already running on your Mac in view: what each one is doing, how much of each plan is left, and a quiet signal when a turn ends.

Overview

{{agentsLead}}, one glance.

Each agent's limits stay apart. Today's and the week's tokens add up across them, and one chart shows the days.

Limits and usage

Know what is left.

Codex shows its weekly allowance, credits and reset times. Claude Code shows its session and weekly limits and today's tokens. Cost is an estimate at API prices, never a bill.

Task cues

A short island.

When a turn starts or finishes, a short island grows from the notch with the project and the request, then folds back by itself. Open the panel right after, and it shows that conversation.

One minute

See it move.

A silent, captioned film rendered from Lunlet's own views on a synthetic Mac: the notch, a task cue, the panel, then the notice on a phone (Feishu) and the reply that continues the session. Playback starts only when you choose it.

Away from your Mac

Step away.
Stay in the loop.

Lock your Mac and go. When a turn finishes, Lunlet sends a short notice to your own {{channels}} bot. One reply, the way that messenger takes it, continues that same Codex session. Approvals always stay on your Mac.

Skins

Make it yours.

Choose {{skins}}, tune the accent color, or import a declarative skin of your own. On a Mac without a notch, the lun·let lettering lives in the menu bar.

Availability

Made for macOS.
Built one step at a time.

Source preview

Codex and Claude Code observation, limits and usage work locally; build Lunlet from source. macOS {{macos}} is the deployment target, and on-device validation currently covers macOS 27 on Apple silicon.

Verified so far

Read-only observation of locally owned Codex and Claude Code records. Locked-Mac notices and one reply per notice are verified on Feishu, Telegram and Slack. Some client workflows still need on-device validation.

Not in the first release

No public macOS download yet; a signed package needs signing and installation checks first. No new mobile conversations, SSH, remote-host discovery or general computer control.
