# Design notes

Presentation rules and algorithm details that the stories in [requirements](requirements.md) rely on. They bind the implementation as the criteria do, change only with the criteria that cite them, and never add behavior a story has not confirmed. Evidence stays in [feasibility](feasibility.md).

## Panel presentation

Refs: US-03, US-04, US-13, US-17, US-19, US-33. The header and the two pages have their own section, [Panel header and pages](#panel-header-and-pages).

- A small blue-purple lettering mark rests close to the left of the unchanged hardware notch, without a black backing; the expanded surface uses native translucent frosted material, restrained branding and compact system typography.
- The default panel shows three complete session rows when the display allows, ending cleanly after the third; further rows scroll. Account, reset, credit and usage information stays above them.
- The hover usage chart floats to the left of the panel, aligned with its bottom, and shows the trend and daily charts without scrolling within the visible screen; screen bounds may require a fallback placement. Token/Cost uses the session tabs' capsule style. The unfolded layout keeps vertical spacing below the hardware notch tight.
- System text styles with nothing below 10 pt and a 4-point spacing scale; status symbols with labels (attention orange, running blue, green only for confirmed success); the expanded panel flush with the top screen edge with concave shoulders, its actions in the notch band and one deep surface without gradients or haze beyond the header's drawn sky; a black task cue continuing the notch; deeper entry lettering on light menu bars; an orange waiting badge; the entry's allowance strand with the 20/40/60/80 and 10% warning marks and no minor ticks; Swift Charts for the usage and trend charts.
- The panel is dark in both appearances, as the notch it hangs from is: dark frosted glass, a deep blue-black fill at half strength over the system's glass — Liquid Glass where available (macOS 26 and later) with a vibrant behind-window material fallback — so the window behind colors it softly while secondary text keeps a contrast of four to one over a white window, and the same color opaque with a border under Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast; its floating chart is dark with it — with smooth spring transitions; every segmented switch (Active/History, Tokens/Cost and the choices in Settings) uses one segmented style, an elevated sliding selection pill that looks identical whether or not the panel is key, in a regular and a small size. The black task-cue island stays opaque (AC-13.08).
- Panel pages use four text roles — title 13 pt semibold, primary 13 pt, secondary 11 pt in the secondary color and a 15 pt semibold figure only for the Overview totals — with one size per line and monospaced digits; the header adds its own figure role; a 4-point grid with 12 pt card padding and section gaps, rows 6 apart and groups 8, so card text, segmented labels and session rows share one leading edge. The page budget stays fixed so choosing a body never resizes the panel; each page's last element fills it instead of leaving blank space.
- The five-hour window (AC-04.15) is a figure in the Codex page's figures row: the window found by its 300-minute duration in either position, its percent left and its reset in the shared wording; a successful read that reports no such window (some plans have only the weekly limit) leaves the figure out and says so in the row's help, and only the absence of any successful read reads Unknown; the vine, the lanterns, the header and the forecast stay weekly.
- Overview daily chart: a line joins each day's total at the top center of its bar; a dashed daily-average line is labeled at its trailing end; hovering a day shows that day's per-agent detail; the totals row shows the other metric on hover (AC-19.07, AC-19.08).

## Panel header and pages

Refs: US-19 (AC-19.09 through AC-19.11), US-04 (AC-04.16 through AC-04.18), US-32 (AC-32.09), US-24 (AC-24.04), US-15, US-31, US-33. Confirmed on 2026-11-13.

- Header: below the notch band a drawn night sky replaces the plain material; it is the only custom-drawn surface, and the page below keeps the system material. One Solar System body stands for each agent, vector art with a fixed look per agent that the skin does not tint, and the Sun in the center stands for all agents. A planet is a globe seen from a little above its equator, with its weather placed by latitude and longitude: belts with uneven edges that bend around it, turbulence inside them, streaks of cloud and storms, all drawn soft, with no sharp marks and no two marks a pair, which on a round body read as a face. The Sun is a light, not a lit ball: the same on every side, white at the core and golden at the limb, which blooms past its edge into a corona of streamers, long and short by turns and an odd number of long ones, so it never reads as a drawn star. Agents keep their Settings order, the first half to the left of the Sun and the rest to its right, each with its name under it. The sky has no edge: it runs from one side of the panel to the other and dissolves into the page's material over a stretch of 162 points that begins among the labels and goes on behind the page's first card. It leaves slowly, so at least four fifths of it is left under the labels' last line; it is about half gone where the page begins and thins out in a long tail, never losing more than about 2% in one point, because a gradient between a dark sky and a light page is seen where it is steepest. Its contours are arcs, so the sides go a little sooner than the middle, and a low dusk tint, indigo then lavender, keeps the last of it from going flat. Only the header's own frame takes clicks. Settings' strip dissolves the same way under its title, over a shorter stretch. A chosen body sits low enough for its disc to clear the notch band; a bright star's glow fades to nothing rather than ending in an edge. Nothing in the header moves by itself: only choosing a body moves anything.
- Lit share: the lit fraction of a body's disc area equals percent left, drawn with an elliptical terminator of half-width R·|2f − 1|; a body left of the Sun is mirrored so its lit side faces the Sun. An unknown limit dims the body under a dark veil, never by transparency, so no star shows through it; its figure is a dash with no unit and the state is written under its name. There is no ring around it and nothing turns.
- Choosing a body: it moves to the center and grows while the others shrink to the sides and keep a short label (percent and name); the sky streaks in the direction of travel for the same 0.72 s. One easing curve; only position, scale and opacity animate; nothing animates while the panel is closed; Reduce Motion shows the end state at once on a still sky. Idle cost is measured before acceptance (US-25).
- Text: the header's figure is 20 points semibold in the row and 22 when centered, one role above the four existing ones; names are 12 points semibold with the plan in the tertiary color; the reset and today lines use the secondary role, so nothing is below 10 points. The figure follows the AC-04.10 warning colors below 20% and 10%.
- More than four agents is not built, because the two implemented agents never reach it; the prototype's rule for later agents keeps the first three in the row beside an entry that counts the rest and opens a grid of every body.
- Overview: the waiting line (AC-19.11) as capsules, the Tokens/Cost totals row (AC-19.07) and the daily chart with its legend (AC-19.08), then one note, "Recorded on this Mac · ≈ estimate · ≥ at least", the only place the two signs are explained.
- Agent page: one card with the account line over the figures, labels beside values in two columns, the second at the trailing edge, or one line when the agent reports a single figure; a value too long for its column ends in an ellipsis with the whole value in help, so the page never lays out wider than the panel; the list header "<Agent> sessions" with the Active/History switch and, on Active only, Clear (AC-15.06); three complete session cards, each with a round badge of one size that carries the state's glyph on a wash of its tone, the title, project and activity, the state in quiet words (in the attention color only while the session waits for the developer), and age, tokens and estimated cost. The list has its own viewport of exactly three whole cards, so no part of a fourth ever shows; further cards scroll inside it while the card and the list header stay. An agent whose top card is shorter leaves room to spare, which its three cards share (48 to 60 points each) instead of showing part of a fourth. A History row that can return to Active offers Restore (AC-15.03).
- Surfaces: on a page, running text sits on a module or a capsule, never straight on the surface, which still takes a little of the color of whatever window lies behind the panel; a list's header and the Overview's totals row, in the primary color beside their switch, are the exceptions.
- Copy: the state under the body and the action on the account line, never the same sentence twice; Unknown for a value that could not be read; an empty place says why and what fills it ("No active Codex sessions. Start one in any terminal and it appears here.", "No history yet. Sessions you clear, and sessions that end, are kept here.", "No usage recorded in the last 5 days"); money in whole dollars from $10 and in cents below, always with the ≈ or ≥ sign `LocalUsageText` writes.
- Floating chart: placement unchanged; the title names the window and the agent with the reset moment; "Now N% left · even pace M%" (AC-32.05), the estimate line (AC-32.09), the trend with the legend So far, Estimate range and Even pace, then Daily usage with the Tokens/Cost switch; an agent with partial records notes that its totals are at least this much.
- Settings (US-31, criteria unchanged): the page opens in the panel's frame under a short strip of the same sky, dissolving the same way, with Back; five labeled icon tabs in the confirmed order; rows grouped under short headings with one note per group saying what it changes; the entry is chosen from three pictured options, and an entry's own options (the menu-bar allowance text, the pet and its packs) show only while it is selected; a page made longer by them scrolls. No setting is added, removed or renamed; Usage gains a read-only group naming the sources its footer and help text state today (AC-04.04).
- Appearance: the panel is dark in both appearances: a dark sky over a light page reads as a picture set on the panel, however it ends. The surface is frosted glass in nearly the color the sky ends in, so the sky is the surface's own darkness with stars in it, and modules are a faint lighter layer with a hairline that is brighter along its top edge, as if lit from above. The chosen body lights the page under it: a wash of its own color, blue for Codex, a warm rose for Claude Code and gold for the Sun and the Overview, pools where the sky gives way to the page and changes with the choice in the same move, so each page has its own light and the dark surface is never flat. Color is kept for what it tells: an agent's body, a state's badge, the accent of a selection, and amber for what waits for the developer. The skin keeps its role, drawing the strand beside the notch and setting the accent color of selections, switches and links; chart segments keep the agent colors of AC-16.03.

## Notch entry and cues

Refs: US-03, US-13, US-17.

- The entry beside the notch is the lettering plus the selected skin's allowance strand (vine, or lanterns under US-17); the vine strand is slightly thicker and gently spiraling along the lettering's lower edge, letter strokes grow into sparse shoots, and both words react together to hover and task cues.
- Menu-bar mode (`MenuBarAllowanceText`): after the lettering and the waiting count, plain text names the window closest to running out among those the cards show (Codex weekly and five-hour, Claude Code weekly), as "25%" or "25% · 1d 9h", prefixed by the agent with several agents and by "5h" for the five-hour window; Off, Percent and Percent and reset, and an Only below level (50/30/20% left) are Settings › Appearance choices; unknown or past-reset windows show no text, and a one-minute timer runs only while a countdown is shown (AC-21.04, AC-21.05).
- Needs you (AC-13.10, AC-13.11): when a verified session enters a question or approval wait on the unlocked Mac, the island (or the pet's cue in pet mode) shows the attention-toned status "Needs you", the project, and "Question · " or "Approval on Mac · " before the bounded prompt preview, for the usual cue duration; the list model numbers each entry into a waiting state per binding, so a record read again is the same entry and a later wait in the same turn is a new one, and the presentation consumes baselines and replays without showing them. The cue router plays the system alert sound once per Needs you cue while Settings › Appearance › Sound for Needs you is on, never for a start or finish. Codex sessions join once the question and approval classification exists; Claude Code's per-process record already names its waits.
- Cues and the panel expand from the resting silhouette, which matches the display's system-reported notch exclusion area; the task cue is a black island continuous with the notch (AC-13.08). Reduce Motion keeps the attached geometry without transitions (AC-03.10).

## Weekly trend chart and pace estimator

Refs: US-32, US-04, US-33.

The chart and the estimator work on the account's own limit window (AC-04.19): the weekly window when the account reports one, otherwise its longest window of a day or more, such as the 30-day window of a plan without a weekly one. The window's length sets the even budget, the lookback and which local records are comparable, and a changed length starts the evidence afresh. A span longer than 16 days is ticked in dates about a fifth of the span apart instead of weekdays, the title names the span ("Month limit · Codex"), and a reset more than six calendar days ahead is written as a date rather than a weekday. The thresholds below were chosen for a seven-day window and are not yet recalibrated for longer ones.

the trend chart shows the selected number of local calendar days of observations, including today, then the forecast through the reported reset, with local midnights as day ticks labeled by weekday and the reset named at the right edge. It draws an observed curve without per-reading symbols over a faint area fill, through readings kept at a four-hour cadence (sooner for a jump of five points, and at the span's ends, day bridges and reset sides) so hourly one-point moves read as one slope, one marker at the latest reading, the forecast as a thin dashed central line with a light range band, and the even budget from 100% at the window's start to 0% at reset as a faint straight guide; 0%, 50% and 100% ticks with hairline grid lines, and a little headroom above 100% so a full allowance is not clipped. Preserve the recorded points immediately before and after each detected reset, and do not invent values for missing dates; forecast inputs do not depend on the displayed range. Forecasts use every comparable official reading from the past seven days at its recorded resolution, including account reads and readings recorded on this Mac, not the daily chart points. They compare changes only within matching reset windows and never count a reset jump, the interval containing it, or time spent at 0% as consumption. For pace only, a reported reset minus seven days is treated as that window's 100% start unless a reading contradicts it; it is never drawn as a reading. Vertical dashed markers show detected reset transitions; the next scheduled reset is the chart's right edge; inferred historical times are explicitly approximate in chart help. Reset timestamps within five minutes are treated as the same window to tolerate observed timestamp drift. Pace is the calendar rate over the past seven days, so daily and weekly patterns average out. When this window's use so far differs from the same hours one week earlier by more than a factor of two, the earlier window is ignored and the estimate is labeled rough until a day of data exists. The forecast starts at the latest comparable reading, assumes no additional reset before the reported reset, and is recalculated when a reading arrives. Hovering a reset line displays its time; inferred times are labeled approximate. Historical rollout records do not identify the account; matching bucket, reset and available plan is not proof of account identity. Missing or inconsistent data does not produce a forecast; AC-32.06 through AC-32.08 define the range, staleness, early-window and exhausted states. The independent estimator does not attribute quota changes to individual models or infer quota from token totals.

## Allowance notifications

Refs: US-41, US-04.

- Settings › Usage › Notifications: the switch (off), Warn below (30, 20, 10 or 5% left; 20 by default) and When a window resets (off). Turning the switch on asks macOS for permission once; a denial shows beside the controls with Open System Settings, and nothing is sent until it is granted. The deliverer is Notification Center only in the bundled app; elsewhere Settings says notifications are unavailable.
- Readings are the ones the cards show: Codex's limit window (weekly, or the longer window an account reports instead, named by its length) and its five-hour window, both by duration, from each completed account read, and Claude Code's weekly window while the status line is connected. A window without a percent or a reset, or past its reset, is no reading, and nothing is read for the purpose (AC-04.05).
- Rule (`AllowanceAlertRule`, per agent and window): a reading below the level warns once; it warns again only after a reading at or above the level or a refreshed window. A reset notice needs a reading taken after the window's reset moment that shows a later reset moment or more allowance than before, once per window; with reset notices off the refresh only re-arms the warning. Reset moments within the estimator's tolerance of each other are the same window, so a drifting timestamp never warns twice; readings of an agent that is not selected are dropped. A reading not newer than the last is ignored. The memory is saved in preferences storage (`allowanceNotifications.windows`), so a relaunch does not repeat a notice.
- Text: "Codex weekly allowance: 18% left" ("monthly" for a 30-day window), "Codex 5-hour window: 9% left", "Claude Code weekly allowance has reset"; the body carries the reset countdown and moment in the cards' wording and nothing about the account. Identifiers are `com.lunlet.allowance.<agent>.<window>.<kind>`, so a repeat replaces its predecessor; a banner shows while Lunlet is active; activating one opens the panel on that agent's page.

## Messaging transports

Refs: US-20, US-36.

- Slack uses Socket Mode and Telegram long polling, for pairing and for replies; Telegram never sets a webhook and preserves an existing one. Listeners run only while a notice can take a reply (AC-36.04): a failing listener is started again after two seconds doubling to a minute for as long as one can, a connection the provider accepted resets that wait, and the Slack socket is pinged so a half-open connection is noticed. A notice's ten minutes are measured on a clock that keeps running while the Mac sleeps. The Feishu connection retries after the jitter alone when a connection that had stayed open drops, after the provider's interval between consecutive failures, and stops only on the provider's verdict on the app's credentials or permissions. On channel change, cancel, disconnect or quit, pending network work is canceled and owned resources are joined before the next channel can act (AC-20.05).

## Claude Code Stop hook

- Claude Code has no command that queues a message into a running session, and its channels need a per-session flag and, during the research preview, a confirmation at every launch. Lunlet uses the Stop hook instead: a `hooks.Stop` entry in Claude Code's user `settings.json`, added and removed by Lunlet like the status line, whose command runs the launcher `~/Library/Application Support/com.lunlet/hook/lunlet-hook` only when it is there (`/bin/sh -c 'if [ -x "$HOME/…" ]; then exec "$HOME/…"; fi'`, so a synced settings file still works and a removed Lunlet folder ends the hook quietly instead of failing the turn end), `timeout` 900 and `statusMessage` "Lunlet: waiting for your phone reply". The launcher runs the `lunlet-hook` helper named in `helper-path`, which the running app publishes, and exits 0 without one. An entry an earlier build wrote (the launcher alone) is still recognized, removed exactly, and replaced in place on the next connect. The status line and the hook are edited through one serial editor, which starts an edit over when the file changed underneath, and Lunlet's script and its record of the previous status line are deleted only once the file is read back without Lunlet's line.
- Folder protocol, under `~/Library/Application Support/com.lunlet/hook/` (user-only): `app.json` (pid and process start time, written only by the Claude source while it runs), so a hook that finds no live app, or a reused pid, exits at once; `stops/<session uuid>/<stop uuid>.stop` written by the helper with the prompt id, `last_assistant_message`, `stop_hook_active`, its pid and parent pid; the app answers `<stop>.hold` or `<stop>.release` within milliseconds (the helper waits at most 3 seconds); while holding, the app may write `<stop>.push` (text) and the helper answers `<stop>.pushed` after printing the continuation. The helper removes its signal and verdict files at exit, also when it is ended by a hangup or termination signal; the app removes the receipt and a dead helper's files, releases a stop of a session it does not track once a scan has looked, and sweeps the folders about every thirty seconds. The reply text in a signal is kept up to the notice's capture size and cut on a character boundary.
- No interface of its own: the Claude Code agent switch adds and removes the hook, the switch's help explains it, and Settings shows a note only when the settings file could not be changed, exactly as the status line behaves; sessions already running pick the entry up.
- Hold rule (AC-38.02): a signal counts only when the helper's parent is the session's sole owning process. Only an interactive session's first completion of the transcript's turn is held. It is held while the reply window is open, which the desktop opens while the Mac is confirmed locked and a channel's sender is configured and closes on unlock or when the sender goes (the same window as the cards), for at most 600 seconds; a hold also ends when the helper exits or Lunlet quits. Only a held turn has the `claudeHook` input path and `alive` liveness, so only its notice carries a reply control.
- Completion: the signal is the completion, with `last_assistant_message` as the conclusion, keyed by `<prompt id>` or `<prompt id>#<n>` after `n` hook continuations; the transcript reader counts Lunlet's continuations from `stop_hook_summary` records whose `hookAdditionalContext` starts with Lunlet's prefix and keys the later `turn_duration` completion the same way, so neither source replays the other's completion (AC-13.04).
- Continuation: the helper prints `{"hookSpecificOutput":{"hookEventName":"Stop","additionalContext":"<prefix><text>"}}`, Claude Code's non-error form, which continues the same turn with the text as the user's next message, labels it hook feedback in the transcript and shows no error; the delivery watch confirms from the first Lunlet record in that transcript. Claude Code caps consecutive hook continuations at eight.

## Desktop pet

- The third entry beside the notch widget and the menu bar (Settings › Appearance › Presentation). Only one entry shows at a time: in pet mode the notch entry and the menu-bar item are hidden, and task cues go to the dog instead of the island.
- Window: a 176 × 152 point floating, non-activating panel on every Space, hidden over a full-screen app, while the Mac is locked and when the display is unavailable; it has no shadow and ignores the pointer except over the dog's silhouette (head, ears, body, paws and the bubble), tracked through mouse-moved monitors, so clicks on transparent space reach the window beneath. A press that moves the window less than three points is a click; a longer one drags it, and when the drag ends its place is saved once, per display number, as an offset from that display's visible corner, together with that display as the pet's display; the default place is the lower trailing corner, 24 points in. Only a drag sets the place: a move the system makes, as when a display goes away, returns the pet to it, and a place an earlier build saved as a global origin is rewritten once while its display is connected. The dog is framed and the pointer followed only while some of the window shows, by the window's occlusion state. At launch and on every display change the pet shows on the display it was last left on while it is connected (for preferences from before that was recorded, the single connected display holding a saved place), else on the display with the focused window, and its saved place there is clamped to the visible area. Whether the display can show the pet is read when the display state changes, never on the pose and gaze updates that arrive many times a second.
- Pose rule (core, `PetPoseRule`): Waiting over Failed over Done over Working over Idle over Asleep across current conversations only (no child tasks, no ended or unconfirmed sessions), the order Codex Pets uses (needs input, blocked, ready, running); Failed and Done last as long as the list model presents the finish cue, the AC-13.02 duration, Failed for a failed outcome and Done for a succeeded or unknown one; a stopped turn shows neither. The state names the session behind the pose, the counts and a compact status line (project · agent · state), which the window shows as its tooltip.
- Drawing (`PetDrawing`): flat shapes in a 220 × 190 design space scaled to the window; sitting poses for Working, Waiting, Done and Failed, lying poses for Idle and Asleep; the Failed dog's head hangs 10 points lower with a flat mouth; ears hang at 14°, perk to 52° for Waiting and Done and lie flat at 0° for Failed; the collar takes the skin's accent and carries a lantern tag lit while Working and Done, pulsing while Waiting, dim while Idle and Failed and off while Asleep; a white bubble with "?", "✓" or "!" sits above the head; three coats (Golden default, Cream, Slate). The lantern's glow is a radial gradient and the bubble glyphs and the sleep marks are stroked shapes, so a frame runs no blur pass and lays out no text.
- Loops: breathing (3.2 s) always, stronger while lying, tail wag (0.9 s) and head bob (1.6 s) while Working, a hop (1.1 s) and twinkling sparkles while Done, drifting "z" letters while Asleep; the Failed dog's tail hangs still and low, so nothing quick runs between blinks. They stop under Reduce Motion, while the window is hidden or the display unavailable; the canvas then redraws only on a state change.
- Click: the pose's session is selected and the companion panel opens as the dog's speech bubble, above the head when it fits and below the dog otherwise, without notch shoulders: a rounded panel whose 14-point tail points at the head (`NotchSurfaceGeometry.bubble`), grown from the tail's tip with the panel's spring and shrunk back into it on close, its content rising out of the dog; while it is open the dog hides its own "?" / "✓" bubble, looks up at the panel and wags eagerly. The secondary button offers Open Lunlet, Refresh, Settings and Quit. Assistive tools read the window as a button labeled with the pose and counts, such as "Lunlet: two sessions, one waiting for you".
- Life between states (`PetMotion`): blinks about every four seconds, sometimes twice; a gaze that wanders and settles on the pointer within 260 points or on the open panel; an ear flick now and then; a slow head tilt; a squash-and-stretch bounce on a click; poses blend over 0.4 s and the bubble pops in over 0.28 s. Everything derives from the time alone, so a frame is a pure function. The frame clock (`PetFrameClock`) is a timer armed for the next frame rather than a SwiftUI timeline, whose display link keeps waking at the screen's rate however few frames it draws. It frames twelve times a second while the dog only loops (AC-39.08), six while it sleeps, and thirty while something quick happens: a blink (none while asleep, when the eyes are closed), an ear flick, a pose transition, a click bounce, the Done hop, or attention from the pointer or the open panel; a calm frame lands exactly on the next blink so none is skipped. It runs only while the window is visible, the display available and Reduce Motion off. A pointer move or a click updates the clock's motion input for the next frame without rebuilding the view. While the dog is dragged, an open bubble follows it.

## Imported pets

- A pet pack is Codex's format read in place (US-40): a folder holding `pet.json` (`id`, `displayName`, `description`, optional `spriteVersionNumber`, `spritesheetPath`) beside a WebP or PNG sheet of 8 columns of 192 × 208 cells, 9 rows (v1, 1536 × 1872) or 11 rows with the 16 look directions (v2, 1536 × 2288). Lunlet lists the folders under `~/Library/Application Support/com.lunlet/pets` and `${CODEX_HOME:-~/.codex}/pets`, reads the manifest and the sheet's header, and names the fault of any folder it cannot use; it never writes into Codex's folder. Preferences keep the choice as `source/folder`; nil is the dog.
- Rows (`PetSpriteRule`): Asleep shows idle's first frame still (with the dog's "z" marks), Idle loops idle, Working loops running, Waiting loops waiting, Done loops review, Failed loops failed; a click plays jumping once; a drag plays running-left or running-right in the direction the window moves; a v2 pack in Idle shows the look cell toward the pointer within the gaze range or toward the open panel (0 is up, clockwise in 22.5° steps, the deadzone falling back to idle). The frame durations are the format's (idle 280/110/110/140/140/320 ms; running rows 120 ms with a longer last frame; waving, jumping and failed 140 ms; waiting and review 150 ms). Reduce Motion shows a row's first frame.
- Drawing (`PetSpriteDrawing`): the cell stands on the window's bottom edge, centered, scaled so that 44 points stay free above it for the bubble; the bubble and sleep marks hang from the top center of the opaque pixels of the resting cell, drawn with the dog's bubble at the dog's scale; the collar and lantern are not drawn. The companion panel's tail points at that top center.
- Hit test: the pointer counts as on the pet over the current cell's opaque pixels (alpha ≥ 24 of 255, sampled from a cached alpha mask per cell) and over the bubble while it shows; elsewhere the window ignores it. The sheet is decoded once off the main thread when the pack is chosen; cells are cut from it on demand.
- Tempo: the frame clock lands on the pack's own frame boundaries, runs at thirty frames a second only while Lunlet's bubble pops in, checks twice a second while a look cell stands still (a pointer move that turns the pet to another look cell redraws it at once) and six times a second while the sleep marks drift; attention from the pointer or the open panel adds no frames of its own.
- Settings › Appearance (pet entry): a Pet picker with the built-in dog and every valid pack as "name · source", Refresh, Open pets folder and Find pets on Petdex (petdex.dev in the browser; `petdex install` writes every pet into Codex's folder too, which the picker's help says, so no third folder is read) beside it, the reason the chosen pack is not showing when it is missing or invalid (the dog shows meanwhile), and each invalid folder with its fault. The Coat picker stays while the dog is chosen.
