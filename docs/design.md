# Design notes

Presentation rules and algorithm details that the stories in [requirements](requirements.md) rely on. They bind the implementation as the criteria do, change only with the criteria that cite them, and never add behavior a story has not confirmed. Evidence stays in [feasibility](feasibility.md).

## Panel presentation

Refs: US-03, US-04, US-13, US-17, US-19, US-33.

- A small blue-purple lettering mark rests close to the left of the unchanged hardware notch, without a black backing; the expanded surface uses native translucent frosted material, restrained branding and compact system typography.
- The default panel shows three complete session rows when the display allows, ending cleanly after the third; further rows scroll. Account, reset, credit and usage information stays above them.
- The hover usage chart floats to the left of the panel, aligned with its bottom, and shows the trend and daily charts without scrolling within the visible screen; screen bounds may require a fallback placement. Token/Cost uses the session tabs' capsule style. The unfolded layout keeps vertical spacing below the hardware notch tight.
- System text styles with nothing below 10 pt and a 4-point spacing scale; status symbols with labels (attention orange, running blue, green only for confirmed success); the expanded panel flush with the top screen edge with concave shoulders, its header in the notch band and plain system material without custom gradients or haze; a black task cue continuing the notch; deeper entry lettering on light menu bars; an orange waiting badge; allowance bars with the 20/40/60/80 and 10% warning marks and no minor ticks; Swift Charts for the usage and trend charts.
- The panel uses translucent frosted glass — Liquid Glass where available (macOS 26 and later) with a vibrant behind-window material fallback, and opaque bordered surfaces under Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast — with smooth spring transitions; every segmented switch (provider tabs and Active/History) uses one segmented style, an elevated sliding selection pill that looks identical whether or not the panel is key, in a regular and a small size. The black task-cue island stays opaque (AC-13.08).
- Panel pages use four text roles — title 13 pt semibold, primary 13 pt, secondary 11 pt in the secondary color and a 15 pt semibold figure only for the Overview totals and each provider card's remaining percentage — with one size per line and monospaced digits; a 4-point grid with 12 pt card padding and section gaps, rows 6 apart and groups 8, so card text, segmented labels and session rows share one leading edge; and one 6 pt meter for every allowance and plan-limit bar. The page budget stays fixed so switching tabs never resizes the panel; each page's last element fills it instead of leaving blank space.
- Overview daily chart: a line joins each day's total at the top center of its bar; a dashed daily-average line is labeled at its trailing end; hovering a day shows that day's per-agent detail; the totals row shows the other metric on hover (AC-19.07, AC-19.08).

## Notch entry and cues

Refs: US-03, US-13, US-17.

- The entry beside the notch is the lettering plus the selected skin's allowance strand (vine, or lanterns under US-17); the vine strand is slightly thicker and gently spiraling along the lettering's lower edge, letter strokes grow into sparse shoots, and both words react together to hover and task cues.

## Weekly trend chart and pace estimator

Refs: US-32, US-04, US-33.

the trend chart shows the selected number of local calendar days of observations, including today, then the forecast through the reported reset, with local midnights as day ticks labeled by weekday and the reset named at the right edge. It draws an observed curve without per-reading symbols over a faint area fill, through readings kept at a four-hour cadence (sooner for a jump of five points, and at the span's ends, day bridges and reset sides) so hourly one-point moves read as one slope, one marker at the latest reading, the forecast as a thin dashed central line with a light range band, and the even budget from 100% at the window's start to 0% at reset as a faint straight guide; 0%, 50% and 100% ticks with hairline grid lines, and a little headroom above 100% so a full allowance is not clipped. Preserve the recorded points immediately before and after each detected reset, and do not invent values for missing dates; forecast inputs do not depend on the displayed range. Forecasts use every comparable official reading from the past seven days at its recorded resolution, including account reads and readings recorded on this Mac, not the daily chart points. They compare changes only within matching reset windows and never count a reset jump, the interval containing it, or time spent at 0% as consumption. For pace only, a reported reset minus seven days is treated as that window's 100% start unless a reading contradicts it; it is never drawn as a reading. Vertical dashed markers show detected reset transitions; the next scheduled reset is the chart's right edge; inferred historical times are explicitly approximate in chart help. Reset timestamps within five minutes are treated as the same window to tolerate observed timestamp drift. Pace is the calendar rate over the past seven days, so daily and weekly patterns average out. When this window's use so far differs from the same hours one week earlier by more than a factor of two, the earlier window is ignored and the estimate is labeled rough until a day of data exists. The forecast starts at the latest comparable reading, assumes no additional reset before the reported reset, and is recalculated when a reading arrives. Hovering a reset line displays its time; inferred times are labeled approximate. Historical rollout records do not identify the account; matching bucket, reset and available plan is not proof of account identity. Missing or inconsistent data does not produce a forecast; AC-32.06 through AC-32.08 define the range, staleness, early-window and exhausted states. The independent estimator does not attribute quota changes to individual models or infer quota from token totals.

## Messaging transports

Refs: US-20, US-36.

- Slack uses Socket Mode and Telegram long polling, for pairing and for replies; Telegram never sets a webhook and preserves an existing one. Listeners run only while a notice can take a reply (AC-36.04); on channel change, cancel, disconnect or quit, pending network work is canceled and owned resources are joined before the next channel can act (AC-20.05).

## Claude Code Stop hook

- Claude Code has no command that queues a message into a running session, and its channels need a per-session flag and, during the research preview, a confirmation at every launch. Lunlet uses the Stop hook instead: a `hooks.Stop` entry in Claude Code's user `settings.json`, added and removed by Lunlet like the status line, whose command is the launcher `~/Library/Application Support/com.lunlet/hook/lunlet-hook` (written as `"$HOME/…"` so a synced settings file still works), `timeout` 900 and `statusMessage` "Lunlet: waiting for your phone reply". The launcher runs the `lunlet-hook` helper named in `helper-path`, which the running app publishes, and exits 0 without one.
- Folder protocol, under `~/Library/Application Support/com.lunlet/hook/` (user-only): `app.json` (pid) while the Claude source runs, so a hook that finds no live app exits at once; `stops/<session uuid>/<stop uuid>.stop` written by the helper with the prompt id, `last_assistant_message`, `stop_hook_active`, its pid and parent pid; the app answers `<stop>.hold` or `<stop>.release` within milliseconds (the helper waits at most 3 seconds); while holding, the app may write `<stop>.push` (text) and the helper answers `<stop>.pushed` after printing the continuation. The helper removes its signal and verdict files at exit; the app removes the receipt, and sweeps folders whose helper is gone.
- No interface of its own: the Claude Code agent switch adds and removes the hook, the switch's help explains it, and Settings shows a note only when the settings file could not be changed, exactly as the status line behaves; sessions already running pick the entry up.
- Hold rule (AC-38.02): a signal counts only when the helper's parent is the session's sole owning process. It is held while the reply window is open, which the desktop opens on lock and closes on unlock (the same window as the cards), for at most 600 seconds; a hold also ends when the helper exits or Lunlet quits. Only a held turn has the `claudeHook` input path and `alive` liveness, so only its notice carries a reply control.
- Completion: the signal is the completion, with `last_assistant_message` as the conclusion, keyed by `<prompt id>` or `<prompt id>#<n>` after `n` hook continuations; the transcript reader counts Lunlet's continuations from `stop_hook_summary` records whose `hookAdditionalContext` starts with Lunlet's prefix and keys the later `turn_duration` completion the same way, so neither source replays the other's completion (AC-13.04).
- Continuation: the helper prints `{"hookSpecificOutput":{"hookEventName":"Stop","additionalContext":"<prefix><text>"}}`, Claude Code's non-error form, which continues the same turn with the text as the user's next message, labels it hook feedback in the transcript and shows no error; the delivery watch confirms from the first Lunlet record in that transcript. Claude Code caps consecutive hook continuations at eight.

## Desktop pet

- The third entry beside the notch widget and the menu bar (Settings › Appearance › Presentation). Only one entry shows at a time: in pet mode the notch entry and the menu-bar item are hidden, and task cues go to the dog instead of the island.
- Window: a 176 × 152 point floating, non-activating panel on every Space, hidden over a full-screen app, while the Mac is locked and when the display is unavailable; it has no shadow and ignores the pointer except over the dog's silhouette (head, ears, body, paws and the bubble), tracked through mouse-moved monitors, so clicks on transparent space reach the window beneath. A press that moves the window less than three points is a click; a longer one drags it, and the origin is saved per display number and clamped to that display's visible area; the default place is the lower trailing corner, 24 points in.
- Pose rule (core, `PetPoseRule`): Waiting over Done over Working over Idle over Asleep across current conversations only (no child tasks, no ended or unconfirmed sessions); Done lasts as long as the list model presents the finish cue, the AC-13.02 duration, and only for a succeeded or unknown outcome. The state names the session behind the pose, the counts and a compact status line (project · agent · state), which the window shows as its tooltip.
- Drawing (`PetDrawing`): flat shapes in a 220 × 190 design space scaled to the window; sitting poses for Working, Waiting and Done, lying poses for Idle and Asleep; ears hang at 14° and perk to 52° for Waiting and Done; the collar takes the skin's accent and carries a lantern tag lit while Working and Done, pulsing while Waiting, dim while Idle and off while Asleep; a white bubble with "?" or "✓" sits above the head; three coats (Golden default, Cream, Slate).
- Loops: breathing (3.2 s) always, stronger while lying, tail wag (0.9 s) and head bob (1.6 s) while Working, a hop (1.1 s) and twinkling sparkles while Done, drifting "z" letters while Asleep. They stop under Reduce Motion, while the window is hidden or the display unavailable; the canvas then redraws only on a state change.
- Click: the pose's session is selected and the companion panel opens as the dog's speech bubble, above the head when it fits and below the dog otherwise, without notch shoulders: a rounded panel whose 14-point tail points at the head (`NotchSurfaceGeometry.bubble`), grown from the tail's tip with the panel's spring and shrunk back into it on close, its content rising out of the dog; while it is open the dog hides its own "?" / "✓" bubble, looks up at the panel and wags eagerly. The secondary button offers Open Lunlet, Refresh, Settings and Quit. Assistive tools read the window as a button labeled with the pose and counts, such as "Lunlet: two sessions, one waiting for you".
- Life between states (`PetMotion`): blinks about every four seconds, sometimes twice; a gaze that wanders and settles on the pointer within 260 points or on the open panel; an ear flick now and then; a slow head tilt; a squash-and-stretch bounce on a click; poses blend over 0.4 s and the bubble pops in over 0.28 s. Everything derives from the time alone, so a frame is a pure function. The timeline (`PetTempoSchedule`) frames twelve times a second while the dog only loops (AC-39.08) and thirty while something quick happens: a blink, an ear flick, a pose transition, a click bounce, the Done hop, or attention from the pointer or the open panel; a calm frame lands exactly on the next blink so none is skipped. It runs only while the window is visible, the display available and Reduce Motion off. A pointer move or a click updates the shared motion input for the next frame without rebuilding the view. While the dog is dragged, an open bubble follows it.
